About

Hello! I am Taenyun Kim (pronounced “Tanyon”), a postdoctoral researcher at the Max Planck Institute for Intelligent Systems.

My research examines the psychology of human–AI and human–robot interaction, with a focus on mental representations, trust, and morality. I study how people understand artificial agents, how trust develops and recovers after errors, and how people judge the morality of AI, robots, and their decisions.

I earned my PhD in Information and Media with a Cognitive Science Specialization at Michigan State University. Previously, I studied Psychology, Informatics, and Human–AI Interaction at Sungkyunkwan University.

I describe myself as a robopsychologist, inspired by Dr. Susan Calvin in Isaac Asimov’s I, Robot. More broadly, I am interested in how technologies shape and extend our minds and bodies.

I welcome research collaborations. Please feel free to get in touch.

Research interests

Mental representations of AI and robots
Mind perception, folk theories, and mental models of artificial agents.
Trust and human–AI cooperation
Trust development, trust violations, and trust repair in human–agent interaction.
Morality and moral judgment
How people evaluate artificial agents and their decisions as part of society.
